## Governance in project finance: best practices

Good governance keeps stakeholders aligned and risks managed so projects finish **on time, within budget and to quality**.

### 1. Execution strategies

- **Planning:** set clear (measurable) objectives; define scope (what the project will and will not deliver, to prevent **scope creep**); build a detailed timeline with milestones and dependencies; allocate resources.
- **Execution phases:** initiation (approval, objectives, funding), planning, execution, monitoring and controlling (track KPIs), closing (final reviews, handover).
- **Scheduling and milestones:** use Gantt charts or PERT; milestones act as checkpoints.
- **Monitoring and reporting:** progress meetings, dashboards, risk reports.
- **Stakeholder engagement:** manage expectations, be transparent, resolve issues early.
- **Quality assurance:** quality control inspections, continuous improvement, independent audits.

### 2. Requirement management

Stakeholder analysis, then **defining requirements** using **SMART** criteria (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) recorded in a **Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)**; prioritise requirements. **Change management** uses a formal change request process and a **Change Control Board**. Traceability links each requirement to tasks, deliverables and tests; **validation** checks deliverables against requirements.

### 3. Procurement management

- **Strategy:** budget constraints, sourcing model (competitive bidding, negotiation), supply-chain logistics.
- **Supplier selection:** prequalification, an open tendering process, evaluating proposals on cost, delivery, quality and past performance.
- **Contract management:** clear terms, performance clauses (penalties), dispute resolution (mediation, arbitration, legal action).
- **Procurement risk:** supplier failure, cost increases, logistics; contingency plans; monitoring. Build long-term supplier relationships.

### 4. Asset and risk management

- **Risk identification:** workshops and brainstorming, a **risk register**, scenario planning, SWOT analysis. Categories: financial, operational, regulatory, environmental, reputational.
- **Risk mitigation:** **avoidance**, **transfer** (insurance, bonds, outsourcing), **reduction** (stronger contracts, buffers, redundancy), **acceptance**.
- **Asset lifecycle:** planning and acquisition, utilisation, maintenance, disposition, value optimisation.
- **Capital investment and return:** financial modelling, cost-benefit analysis, financial audits, ROI tracking.
- **Regular risk reviews:** periodic assessments, risk audits, lessons-learned feedback loops.

## Investors in major projects

| Investor type | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Equity investors** | Provide capital for ownership and a share of returns | Project sponsors, private equity firms, institutional investors (pension funds, insurers). Highest risk. Exit through sale of the project, IPO, or M&A. |
| **Debt investors** | Provide loans or bonds, expect fixed returns | Commercial banks, development finance institutions (DFIs), bondholders, syndicated loans, structured finance (securitisation). |
| **Government entities** | Investors or enablers | Subsidies, grants, loans and guarantees, tax relief, **public-private partnerships (PPP)**. |
| **Strategic investors** | Invest for business reasons beyond return | Large corporations, sector-specific investors (energy firms funding renewables). |

**PPP models:** **DBFO** (Design-Build-Finance-Operate) and **BOT** (Build-Operate-Transfer, where the private company runs the project for a period and then transfers it to the government). In PPPs, risk is shared: government takes political, regulatory and land risks; the private partner takes construction, financing and operating risks.

## Organisational design for large projects

- **Governance structure:** a **Steering Committee** (strategic direction, major decisions, resolves conflicts), a **Project Management Office (PMO)** (documentation, standard processes, KPIs, communication), the **project manager** (day-to-day), and specialised teams (risk, procurement, legal).
- **Roles and responsibilities:** clear roles prevent duplication and give accountability.
- **Team composition:** cross-functional teams, skills development, team building.
- **Communication strategy:** clear channels, internal and external communication, transparent reporting, conflict resolution, regular updates, feedback loops.

## Resource management

**Resource management** is the planning, allocation, use and monitoring of resources to reach objectives efficiently. Resources are **human, financial, physical, technological and time**.

**Why it matters:** optimises utilisation; saves cost; improves project delivery; reduces risk; improves teamwork; supports growth and sustainability.

**Challenges:** scarcity; allocation conflicts; changing requirements; forecasting; overburdened resources; data and technology problems; remote and global teams; regulation; sustainability and ethics.

### Types of project resources

- **Human:** match skills to tasks, share workload, communicate, motivate, train.
- **Financial:** budget planning and control, cost estimation, contingency fund, financial reporting.
- **Physical:** procurement and supply chain, maintenance and utilisation, storage and inventory.
- **Technological:** technology selection, IT support, security and data management.
- **Time:** task scheduling (critical path), prioritisation, time tracking, handling delays.

### Resource Management Plan (RMP)

A living part of the project management plan covering: resource planning and identification; acquisition (procurement, hiring, outsourcing); allocation (task matching, **resource levelling**, timeline integration); monitoring and control (KPIs, utilisation reviews, corrective action); resource risk management; communication and reporting.

### Corporate resource management across portfolios and programmes

- A **project** is a temporary effort to create a unique product, service or result. A **programme** is a group of related projects managed together for benefits not available individually. A **portfolio** is a collection of programmes, projects and other work aligned with strategy.
- Managing resources at portfolio level aligns resources with strategy, optimises utilisation, improves decisions, reduces risk and cost, and balances demand and supply.
- **Components:** resource planning (identification, scheduling, allocation); optimisation (utilisation monitoring, resource pooling); prioritisation; monitoring and KPIs; risk and conflict resolution; communication; performance evaluation and post-implementation review.

### Tools and techniques

- **Tools:** Microsoft Project, Trello, Smartsheet, Asana, Monday.com.
- **Capacity planning:** forecast demand, check availability, balance the load, adjust continuously.
- **Utilisation:** human, equipment and material utilisation.
- **Scheduling resources:** task allocation, resolving conflicts, Gantt charts and **resource histograms**.
- **Resource levelling:** adjust the schedule to remove overload; may extend the project. **Resource smoothing:** keep resource use within limits **without** changing the project end date.
- **Efficiency measures:** resource utilisation, revenue per employee, **cost variance (CV)** and **schedule variance (SV)** (positive CV = under budget; positive SV = ahead of schedule).
