## What is a word class?

"Part of speech" is a traditional grammar term. Modern grammarians prefer **word class**, because not every word class takes part in speech in the same way, and clauses and groups can also claim to be "parts" of speech.

English has **8 word classes**:

1. Noun
2. Pronoun
3. Verb
4. Adjective
5. Preposition
6. Conjunction
7. Adverb
8. Articles / determiners

### Lexical items and grammatical items

- **Lexical items** have clear lexical content, a clear meaning of their own: **nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs.**
- **Grammatical items** are used mainly for grammatical operations. They work like "grammatical pebbles" holding a sentence together rather than carrying independent meaning: **pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions and articles.**

## 1. Noun

A noun is a word used for naming a **person** (Mohammed), **animal** (cat), **place** (Lagos), **object** (book), **substance** (gas), **quality** (wisdom), **action** (boxing) or **measure** (inch).

### Functions of a noun in a sentence

- **Head word of a nominal (noun) group**, which functions as subject, object or complement in a clause. *Tayo (S) greeted (V) a student (O) with respect.*
- **Qualifier in a prepositional group.** *The book is for Ojo.*
- **Subject complement.** *The president is Bola Ahmed Tinubu. Bisi and Mary are students.*
- **Object.** *I like fruits. Bimbo gave Femi a fruit.*
- **Apposition** (a noun placed beside another to rename it). *The Oba of Lagos, Akinolu, will be in attendance.*

Note: **proper nouns** (names of particular persons, places, etc.) are always capitalised.

### Types of noun

Two broad classes:

- **Proper noun** - the name of a particular person, place or thing (Mohammed, Lagos).
- **Common noun** - names a class of persons, places, animals or things (boy, city, dog).

Common nouns divide into **count (countable)** and **non-count (uncountable)** nouns, and each of these can be **concrete** or **abstract**.

- **Count nouns** refer to separable things you can count (book, student).
  - **Regular** count nouns form the plural by adding **-s or -es** (book - books).
  - **Irregular** count nouns do not follow the pattern and must be learnt individually (tooth - teeth, child - children, man - men).
- **Non-count nouns** cannot be counted as separate units (water, advice, information).
- **Concrete nouns** can be perceived by the senses (table, rice). **Abstract nouns** name ideas or qualities (honesty, wisdom).

## 2. Verb

The verb is a very significant word class; it forms the **core of every grammatical structure.**

A verb is not just an "action word". Compare:

- *She **is** a teacher.* - shows identity, not action.
- *The man **seems** angry.* - shows a condition.
- *The child **grew** taller.* - shows a change of state.

So a verb describes an **action, a state or a process.**

### Subclasses of verb

The lecture note gives three main classes:

1. **Auxiliary verbs**
2. **Lexical verbs**
3. A third class written in the handout as "catelative" verbs. The examples used (*is, seems, grew*) are **copular (linking) verbs**, which link a subject to a description or identity. Check with your lecturer for the exact term he wants in exams.

### Auxiliary verbs

Called auxiliary ("helping") because they need a **lexical verb** to function in the verbal group. They do not usually stand alone as the verb of a sentence.

There are two subsets:

- **Primary auxiliaries**
  - **Be forms:** is, am, are, was, were, be, been, being
  - **Have forms:** has, have, had (as in *have been*)
  - **Do forms:** do, does, did
- **Modal auxiliaries:** will, shall, can, may, could, would, should, might, must

The main job of auxiliaries is to help express **tense and aspect** in the verbal group.

### Lexical verbs

All verbs that serve as the **main verb** in a structure are lexical verbs. They carry the main action or meaning: dance, eat, write.

- *I **dance** every day.*
- *I **went** to Lagos last week.*

Lexical verbs are either **regular** or **irregular**, depending on how the past tense and past participle are formed.

- A **regular verb** forms both by adding **-ed** (or **-d**) to the base form.
- An **irregular verb** does not follow this rule; its forms vary.

| Base (present) | Past | Past participle | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| jump | jumped | jumped | regular |
| dance | danced | danced | regular |
| sing | sang | sung | irregular |
| drive | drove | driven | irregular |
| hit | hit | hit | irregular |
| write | wrote | written | irregular |
| go | went | gone | irregular |
| eat | ate | eaten | irregular |

## 3. Adjective

An adjective **describes or modifies a noun** in a grammatical structure, adding to its meaning.

Types listed in the lecture note:

- **Adjective of quality:** beautiful, short, small. *A beautiful girl.*
- **Interrogative adjective:** asks a question about a noun: which, what, whose. *Which book do you want?*
- **Nominal adjective:** a noun used as an adjective. *A school uniform, a chicken farm.*
- **Quantitative adjective:** tells how much or how many: some, many, three. *Three students.*
- **Participial adjective:** a participle used as an adjective. *A broken window, a running tap.*

## 4. Pronoun (supplementary)

A pronoun **takes the place of a noun** so we do not repeat it. It is a grammatical item.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Personal | I, you, he, she, it, we, they, me, him, her, us, them |
| Possessive | mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs |
| Reflexive | myself, yourself, himself, herself, themselves |
| Demonstrative | this, that, these, those |
| Interrogative | who, whom, whose, which, what |
| Relative | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| Indefinite | someone, anybody, everything, none, each |

*Ada said that she would come.* (*she* replaces *Ada*)

## 5. Adverb (supplementary)

An adverb modifies a **verb, an adjective or another adverb.** It is a lexical item.

- **Manner:** *She sings **beautifully**.*
- **Time:** *He came **yesterday**.*
- **Place:** *They live **here**.*
- **Frequency:** *I **always** read at night.*
- **Degree:** *It is **very** hot.*

## 6. Preposition (supplementary)

A preposition shows the **relationship** (place, time, direction, etc.) between a noun or pronoun and another word. It is a grammatical item.

- **Place:** in, on, under, behind, between - *The book is **on** the table.*
- **Time:** at, in, on, before, after, during - *We meet **at** noon.*
- **Direction:** to, from, into, towards - *He ran **to** the gate.*

## 7. Conjunction (supplementary)

A conjunction **joins** words, phrases or clauses. It is a grammatical item.

- **Coordinating** conjunctions join equal parts: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so. *I wanted to go, **but** it rained.*
- **Subordinating** conjunctions join a dependent clause to a main clause: because, although, if, when, since, while. *She stayed home **because** she was ill.*

## 8. Articles and determiners (supplementary)

Determiners come before a noun to show which one or how many.

- **Articles:** the **definite** article *the*; the **indefinite** articles *a* and *an* (use *an* before a vowel **sound**: *an hour, a university*).
- **Demonstratives:** this, that, these, those.
- **Possessives:** my, your, his, her, our, their.
- **Quantifiers:** some, any, many, few, several.
