## More techniques of writing

The Writing note covers punctuation and spelling. Further good habits:

- **Spelling:** check commonly confused words: *their / there / they're*, *its / it's*, *affect / effect*, *principal / principle*, *accept / except*, *advice / advise*. Use a dictionary when unsure.
- **Capital letters:** start of a sentence, proper nouns, days, months, titles, the pronoun *I*.
- **Paragraphing:** one main idea per paragraph.
- **Revising:** read your work aloud, then check content, organisation, grammar and punctuation. Good writing is rewritten.

## The paragraph

A paragraph is a group of sentences developing **one main idea.**

1. **Topic sentence** - states the main idea (usually first).
2. **Supporting sentences** - explain, give reasons, examples, facts.
3. **Concluding sentence** - rounds off or links to the next paragraph.

A good paragraph has **unity** (everything relates to the main idea), **coherence** (ideas flow logically, with linking words such as *firstly, moreover, however, therefore, for example, in conclusion*) and **adequate development.**

## The essay

An essay has three main parts:

- **Introduction** - introduces the topic and gives your position or thesis.
- **Body** - several paragraphs, each developing one point with support.
- **Conclusion** - summarises the points and leaves a final thought. Do not introduce a new idea here.

**Steps:** understand the question, brainstorm, outline, write a draft, revise, proofread.

**Types of essay**
- **Narrative** - tells a story or sequence of events.
- **Descriptive** - paints a picture of a person, place or object.
- **Expository** - explains or informs (how something works, why something happens).
- **Argumentative** - persuades the reader to accept a position, with reasons and evidence.

This matches the purposes of writing in the Writing note: to tell a sequence of events, explain how something is made, explain the reason for something, compare, or seek advice.

## Letter writing

**Formal letter layout** (to an official, employer, institution)

1. Sender's address (top right)
2. Date
3. Receiver's name/title and address (left)
4. Salutation: *Dear Sir / Dear Madam / Dear Mr Bello*
5. Heading (title/subject line, often underlined or in capitals)
6. Body: introduction (reason for writing), main message, conclusion (what you expect)
7. Closing: *Yours faithfully* (when you began with Dear Sir/Madam) or *Yours sincerely* (when you used a name)
8. Signature and full name

**Informal letter** (to a friend or relative): sender's address and date, *Dear Tunde*, a chatty and personal body, and a close such as *Yours*, *Your friend* or *With love*.

**Tips:** keep a formal letter polite, direct and brief; use correct grammar and punctuation; never use slang or text-message spelling.

## Summary writing

A summary presents the **main points** of a passage in your **own words** and in fewer words.

1. Read the passage carefully (at least twice).
2. Underline the main points; leave out examples and repetition.
3. Write the points in your own words, in complete sentences.
4. Keep to the word limit and to the order of the passage.
