## 1. Tense

Tense shows **when** an action happens. Aspect shows whether it is ongoing or complete.

### Present
| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple present | habits, facts, routines | She **writes** every day. Water **boils** at 100 degrees Celsius. |
| Present continuous | happening now | They **are reading** now. |
| Present perfect | past action with a present link | I **have finished** my assignment. |
| Present perfect continuous | began in the past and continues | He **has been studying** since morning. |

### Past
| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple past | finished action at a definite time | We **went** to Ikeja yesterday. |
| Past continuous | ongoing at a past moment | I **was cooking** when she called. |
| Past perfect | earlier than another past action | The class **had started** before I arrived. |
| Past perfect continuous | ongoing until a past moment | She **had been waiting** for an hour. |

### Future
| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple future | prediction, decision | I **will call** you tomorrow. |
| Future continuous | ongoing at a future time | This time next week I **will be writing** exams. |
| Future perfect | complete before a future time | By June he **will have graduated**. |

**Common errors**
- *He don't know.* -> *He **doesn't** know.* (third person singular takes does/doesn't)
- *I have seen him yesterday.* -> *I **saw** him yesterday.* (use simple past with a definite past time)
- *She said she will come.* -> *She said she **would** come.* (backshift in reported speech)

## 2. Modal verbs

Modals are auxiliary verbs that express **ability, permission, possibility, obligation, advice** or **prediction.** They are followed by the **base form** of the verb and take no -s in the third person.

| Modal | Main uses | Example |
|---|---|---|
| can | ability, permission (informal) | She **can** swim. **Can** I go? |
| could | past ability, polite request | He **could** read at four. **Could** you help me? |
| may | permission, possibility | You **may** leave. It **may** rain. |
| might | weaker possibility | He **might** be late. |
| will | future, willingness | I **will** help you. |
| would | polite request, past habit, conditional | **Would** you sit down? If I knew, I **would** tell you. |
| shall | offers and suggestions (first person) | **Shall** we begin? |
| should | advice, expectation | You **should** revise daily. |
| must | strong obligation, logical certainty | You **must** register. He **must** be home. |
| ought to | moral duty or advice | We **ought to** respect elders. |

Correct: *She can sing.* Wrong: *She can sings.* / *She can to sing.*

## 3. Concord (subject-verb agreement)

**Rule:** the verb must agree in number (singular or plural) with its **subject.**

1. **Singular subject takes a singular verb; plural takes plural.** *The boy runs. The boys run.*
2. **Words between subject and verb do not change the verb.** *The **teacher**, together with her students, **is** here.* (the subject is *teacher*)
3. **Subjects joined by *and* are plural.** *Ada and Bayo **are** friends.* Exception: one idea or person - *Bread and butter **is** my breakfast.*
4. **Subjects joined by *or / nor / either...or / neither...nor*:** the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. *Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present. Neither the students nor the teacher **was** present.*
5. **Collective nouns** (class, team, committee, family) take a singular verb when seen as one unit, plural when members act separately. *The class **is** large. The committee **are** divided.*
6. **Each, every, either, neither, one, anybody, everybody, someone, nobody** take a **singular** verb. *Each of the students **has** a book.*
7. **Nouns that look plural but are singular:** news, mathematics, physics, politics, economics, athletics. *The news **is** good. Mathematics **is** difficult.*
8. **Nouns that are always plural:** trousers, scissors, glasses, police, cattle. *The police **are** here.*
9. ***A number of* takes plural; *the number of* takes singular.** *A number of students **are** absent. The number of students **is** growing.*
10. **There is / there are:** the verb agrees with the noun that follows. *There **is** a book. There **are** two books.*
11. **Amounts of time, money, distance** are singular units. *Five thousand naira **is** too much.*
12. **Relative clauses:** the verb agrees with the noun the pronoun refers to. *She is one of the students who **work** hard.*
