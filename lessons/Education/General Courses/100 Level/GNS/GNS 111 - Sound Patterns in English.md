## Letters are not sounds

English spelling does not match pronunciation. *Phonetics* studies **speech sounds**, not letters.

- "knife" has 5 letters but 3 sounds: /naɪf/
- "ship" has 4 letters but 3 sounds: /ʃɪp/
- "x" in "box" stands for two sounds: /ks/

English has about **44 sounds** (in RP): **20 vowels** and **24 consonants.**

## Vowels (20)

A vowel is a sound made with **no blocking** of the airstream in the mouth.

**12 pure vowels (monophthongs)**

| Short | Long |
|---|---|
| /ɪ/ sit | /iː/ seat |
| /e/ bed | /ɜː/ bird |
| /æ/ cat | /ɑː/ car |
| /ɒ/ hot | /ɔː/ door |
| /ʊ/ put | /uː/ food |
| /ʌ/ cup | |
| /ə/ about (the weak "schwa") | |

**8 diphthongs** (glides from one vowel to another)

- Closing: /eɪ/ day, /aɪ/ my, /ɔɪ/ boy, /əʊ/ go, /aʊ/ now
- Centring: /ɪə/ here, /eə/ hair, /ʊə/ pure

## Consonants (24)

A consonant is made with **some blocking or narrowing** of the airstream. Describe each by three things: **voicing, place and manner.**

- **Voicing:** voiced (vocal cords vibrate: /b d g v z/) or voiceless (/p t k f s/). Test by touching your throat.
- **Place of articulation:** bilabial (lips: /p b m/), labiodental (lip and teeth: /f v/), dental (/θ ð/), alveolar (/t d s z n l/), palato-alveolar (/ʃ ʒ tʃ dʒ/), palatal (/j/), velar (/k g ŋ/), glottal (/h/).
- **Manner of articulation:** plosive (/p b t d k g/), fricative (/f v θ ð s z ʃ ʒ h/), affricate (/tʃ dʒ/), nasal (/m n ŋ/), lateral (/l/), approximant (/r w j/).

Examples: /p/ = voiceless bilabial plosive. /z/ = voiced alveolar fricative.

**Minimal pairs** differ by only one sound and show that the sounds matter: *ship / sheep*, *bit / beat*, *pat / bat*, *thin / sin*.

## Syllables

A **syllable** has one vowel sound, with or without consonants around it. *teacher* = 2 syllables (tea-cher). *examination* = 5 (ex-am-i-na-tion).

## Word stress

In words of more than one syllable, **one syllable is said with more force, length and loudness.** Marked with ' before the stressed syllable.

- **Two-syllable nouns and adjectives** are usually stressed on the first syllable: *'teacher, 'table, 'happy.*
- **Two-syllable verbs** are often stressed on the second: *re'peat, de'cide, be'gin.*
- **Stress can change the word class:** *'record* (noun) vs *re'cord* (verb); *'present* (noun) vs *pre'sent* (verb); *'import / im'port*.
- **Words ending in -tion, -sion, -ic, -ical, -ity** are stressed on the syllable **before** the ending: *edu'cation, e'conomy, e'conomic, a'bility.*
- Unstressed syllables often contain the weak vowel **/ə/**: *a'bout, 'teacher.*

## Sentence stress and rhythm

In a sentence, **content words** (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs) are stressed; **function words** (articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, pronouns) are usually weak. English is stress-timed: stressed syllables come at roughly regular intervals.

*The 'BOY is 'READing a 'BOOK.*

## Intonation

Intonation is the **rise and fall of the voice** across a sentence.

- **Falling tone** - statements, commands, wh-questions: *I'm going home.* (falls) *Where do you live?* (falls)
- **Rising tone** - yes/no questions, uncertainty: *Are you ready?* (rises)
- **Lists:** rise on each item and fall on the last: *We bought rice, beans, yam and garri.*
- Intonation can change meaning or attitude: *Really?* (rising = surprise).

## Practice

1. How many sounds are in the word *church*? (Answer: 3 - /tʃɜːtʃ/)
2. Which word has a different vowel sound: *beat, seat, bit, meet*? (Answer: *bit*)
3. Mark the stress: *begin, teacher, education.* (Answers: be'gin, 'teacher, edu'cation)
