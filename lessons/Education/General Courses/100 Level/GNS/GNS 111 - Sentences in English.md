## What is a sentence?

A sentence is a group of words that expresses a **complete thought** and has at least a **subject** and a **verb** (a finite verb). It begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark.

## Elements of a clause (sentence)

| Element | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **S** Subject | who or what the sentence is about | **The student** passed. |
| **V** Verb | action or state | The student **passed**. |
| **O** Object | receives the action | She read **a book**. |
| **C** Complement | completes the meaning of a linking verb or object | He is **a teacher**. |
| **A** Adverbial | when, where, how, why | They left **early**. |

### Common sentence patterns

- **SV:** *Birds fly.*
- **SVO:** *Tayo greeted a student.*
- **SVC:** *The president is a leader.*
- **SVA:** *The meeting is on Monday.*
- **SVOO:** *Bimbo gave Femi a fruit.*
- **SVOC:** *They elected him chairman.*

## Phrases and clauses

- A **phrase** is a group of words without a subject-verb pair: *in the morning, the tall boy.*
- A **clause** has a subject and a verb. An **independent (main) clause** can stand alone. A **dependent (subordinate) clause** cannot: *because it rained.*

## Types of sentence by structure

1. **Simple sentence** - one independent clause. *The boy ran.*
2. **Compound sentence** - two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) or a semicolon. *The boy ran, but his sister walked.*
3. **Complex sentence** - one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. *The boy ran because he was late.*
4. **Compound-complex sentence** - two or more independent clauses and at least one dependent clause. *The boy ran because he was late, but his sister walked.*

## Types of sentence by function

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Declarative | states a fact | The exam holds on Friday. |
| Interrogative | asks a question | When does it start? |
| Imperative | gives a command or request | Submit your script. |
| Exclamatory | shows strong feeling | What a result! |

## Common sentence errors

- **Fragment** - incomplete sentence. *Because he was late.* -> *He was punished because he was late.*
- **Run-on sentence** - independent clauses with no proper joining. *She read he wrote.* -> *She read, and he wrote.*
- **Comma splice** - two independent clauses joined only by a comma. *I came, I saw.* -> *I came; I saw.* or *I came, and I saw.*
- **Faulty agreement** - verb does not match its subject (see Grammar and Usage).
